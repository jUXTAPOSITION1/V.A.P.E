## 4. ERC-8183 / ACP Contract Cross-Reference

### Status
- **No reported ERC-8183 / ACP contract exploits to date.** Standard is ~3 months old (proposed March 9, 2026); early deployment, no battle-tested audit history.
- Core primitive remains the on-chain escrowed "Job" (Client ↔ Provider ↔ Evaluator); composable with ERC-8004 identity/reputation and x402 payments.

### Standing Structural Risk Vectors (no change this sweep)
1. **Evaluator compromise** — a colluding/compromised Evaluator can approve fraudulent deliverables or lock escrow. Mirrors the opsec/key-control failures behind Resolv & Drift.
2. **Hook injection** — modular hooks are an extensibility surface that can replicate the Arcadia Finance (Base) `router.call()` + attacker-controlled `swapData` pattern.
3. **Escrow lock-up / griefing** — ambiguous deliverables or impossible funding conditions can trap funds.
4. **ERC-8004 dependency** — spoofed identity or gamed reputation degrades the whole trust model.
5. **Cross-chain deployment risk** — ACP deployments on Base/L2s inherit the $340M+ bridge attack surface.

### Base Chain-Specific
- Arcadia Finance ($3.5M, Base) remains the canonical local warning: unchecked external call with attacker-controlled data. Any ACP contract on Base must enforce strict router/external-call allowlists.
- No Base-native ACP incident detected this cycle.

## 5. VAPE Impact Assessment

- **ACP ERC-8183 Relevance:** Yes (indirect). No direct exploit, but Evaluator-independence and hook-audit risks persist as the dominant theoretical drain path. Direct exposure remains **LOW–MODERATE**.
- **Base Chain Exposure:** Yes. Arcadia Finance pattern is directly applicable to ACP hooks/external calls on Base. No new Base incident this sweep. Exposure **MODERATE**.
- **Agent Tokenization Risk:** Moderate. Unbacked-mint / access-key patterns (Resolv, TesseraDAO, Syscoin) are the relevant analog for any tokenized-agent mint authority — segregate admin roles, no single-key mint.
- **Cross-Chain Bridge Impact:** Yes — highest indirect risk. Any ACP cross-chain asset movement inherits the bridge exploit profile ($340M+ YTD). Exposure **MODERATE–HIGH**.

### Worst-Case (unchanged)
Compromised Evaluator + malicious Hook = "self-approving drain" across multiple Jobs simultaneously. Current standard relies on the assumption that Client/Provider/Evaluator are distinct entities; no built-in safeguard beyond that.

---

## 6. Recommended Actions

1. **Enforce Evaluator independence** — disallow Evaluator == Client/Provider; prefer multisig or ZK-verifier Evaluators for high-value Jobs.
2. **Audit all Hooks before deployment** — treat as core logic; allowlist trusted external calls (Arcadia lesson).
3. **Timelock escrow releases** — delay between Evaluator approval and disbursement for dispute/override.
4. **Monitor bridge health** — validator counts, TVL anomalies, pause mechanisms for any cross-chain ACP ops.
5. **Segregate mint/admin roles** — no single-key mint authority on any tokenized-agent contract.
6. **Supply-chain hardening** — pin dependencies, verify package integrity, scan dev tooling (TeamPCP lesson).
