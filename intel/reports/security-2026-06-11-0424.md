## Key Attack Trend Analysis

### 1. Shift from Code Bugs to Key Compromise
72% of 2026 losses come from stolen keys and credential theft — not smart contract vulnerabilities. The Wasabi exploit on Base proved that even well-audited contracts are defenseless if the deployer key is single-sig and unprotected.

### 2. Cross-Chain Bridges Remain the #1 Attack Surface
Kelp DAO ($292M), THORChain ($10.7M), IoTeX ($4.4M), and others confirm that bridge infrastructure is the highest-value target. The "Finality Gap" / CCSC attack pattern is emerging as a novel vector.

### 3. Social Engineering at Scale
The Drift Protocol attack ($285M) involved months of relationship building by a North Korean hacking group posing as a quant trading firm. This is not a technical vulnerability — it's an operational security failure.

### 4. AI-Assisted Exploit Development
LLMs are now being used to identify vulnerability patterns at scale, accelerating the pace of exploit discovery against unverified contracts.

### 5. Governance as Attack Vector
The Moonwell incident ($1,800 → near $1.08M drainage) shows that governance token quorums can be weaponized trivially if token distribution is weak.
