- Agrees with the verdict above:

The evidence paints CATE as a long-running Uniswap token (nearly two years of continuous pricing on an independent aggregator) whose contract has been deliberately stripped of upgrade or mint authority and whose liquidity has been fully locked. Those controls, combined with a zero-address owner and zero taxes, create a profile consistent with an older meme or community token that survived the post-2022 cleanup phase rather than a fresh deployment built for exit. The 7333-holder base and 12 kB custom verified bytecode further support that this is not a factory clone being spun up for a quick flip.

The 667 % 24 h move on roughly 7× daily volume relative to the existing liquidity pool is the only live anomaly. Because liquidity is locked and supply cannot be inflated, the move cannot be a classic rug, but it does expose how thin the order book remains after years of low activity; a coordinated buy or single large accumulation can still produce exactly this price action without violating any on-chain guardrails.

The rule-based score correctly credits the structural longevity signals but appears to treat the volatility flag as a generic risk rather than a specific liquidity-depth problem. With only ~$100 k locked and volume already exceeding that figure in a single day, the same renounced/locked setup that prevents theft simultaneously enables outsized, low-friction pumps that can reverse just as sharply.

Next step: pull the 24 h transaction list and cluster the largest buys by funding source and wallet age to determine whether the spike is retail FOMO or a small set of coordinated wallets.
