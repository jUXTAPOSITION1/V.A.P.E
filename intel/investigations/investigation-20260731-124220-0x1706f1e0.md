## Expert Assessment

The evidence paints MarsCoin as the latest in a serial launch pattern from the same deployer behind HEYI. Both contracts share the same anonymous creator wallet, with MarsCoin using a custom (non-factory) verified source and renounced ownership to project legitimacy, while the pair remains under a month old with only ~$54k locked liquidity. The 1438-holder count looks superficially broad until cross-referenced with the 89% top-10 concentration: this distribution pattern is typical of controlled “community” launches where a handful of wallets (often linked to the deployer) retain exit power even after renouncement. Zero taxes, non-mintable status, and no honeypot flags are consistent with a post-rug cleanup phase rather than organic development, and the modest 24h volume relative to liquidity does not offset the structural control risk.

The rule-based score overweighted the raw holder count while underweighting how directly the 89% concentration contradicts any claim of broad distribution; it also treated the prior HEYI verdict as one additive factor rather than evidence of a repeatable playbook (same deployer, similar token metadata style, renounced-but-concentrated structure). The 26-day pair age is correctly flagged but gains extra weight when paired with the serial history, because it shows the deployer is iterating quickly rather than iterating on a single project.

Next cycle or human check should pull the full transaction graph of the deployer wallet across both contracts to see whether any large unlocks or transfers from HEYI top holders map to current MarsCoin top holders.
