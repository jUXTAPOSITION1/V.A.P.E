## Expert Assessment
- Agrees with the verdict above:

The evidence points to an older token (first priced on DefiLlama ~270 days ago) that only recently received a fresh liquidity pair, most likely a migration or relisting rather than a true zero-day launch. The renounced ownership and custom (non-template) verified contract are consistent with a project that has survived long enough to iterate its deployment, and the zero taxes plus non-honeypot status align with a functional trading token rather than an obvious rug vector. However, the mint flag remaining active despite the zero-address owner creates a direct contradiction: either the mint logic is callable by another mechanism (e.g., a privileged role not transferred) or the contract was deployed with an intentional backdoor that renunciation did not close. Combined with only 57 holders, 57 % supply in the top-10 wallets, and just $26 k liquidity, the setup still matches the profile of a low-float token that can be moved sharply by a small group even if the original team is no longer in control.

The rule-based score overweighted the 2.3-day pair age as “extreme fresh-launch risk” while underweighting the independent 270-day pricing history; those two facts together indicate the risk is not launch-related but rather re-launch or liquidity-migration risk. It also treated mintable=1 as a blanket dilution flag without noting that the same renunciation that scores positively elsewhere may or may not have disabled minting—an ambiguity the on-chain data alone cannot resolve.

Next step: read the verified contract source (especially the mint function and any role checks) to determine whether minting remains executable after renunciation, and cross-check the top-10 holder addresses against the prior 270-day trading history for signs of coordinated accumulation.
