## Expert Assessment
- Agrees with the verdict above:

The evidence paints H420 as a low-cap, long-running meme-style token (name and 420 motif imply cannabis/humor branding) that launched with custom Solidity code rather than a clone factory. It has sustained Uniswap trading for roughly 16 months at negligible volume with zero taxes, no mint capability, and no honeypot behavior, which together suggest the deployer deliberately avoided the most obvious exit-scam mechanics. Yet the same data set shows the original deployer still controls the contract, 95 % of supply sits with the top 10 wallets, and the entire liquidity pool remains fully unlocked—conditions that have historically enabled rugs even on older tokens. The 488-day “clean” history is therefore less reassuring than it first appears: it may simply reflect that the concentrated holders have not yet chosen to exit rather than any structural safety.

The rule-based score correctly flags the liquidity and concentration risks but under-weights the tension between “custom verified source” and “owner still active.” A hand-written contract that has never been upgraded or minted into is a modest positive signal; leaving the owner unrenounced after more than a year is a stronger negative one, because it keeps the option to change those parameters open. The low holder count (97) and $23 k liquidity amplify that asymmetry rather than offset it.

Next concrete check: pull the verified source on Etherscan and inspect the owner-only functions (especially any that could still affect liquidity or fees) together with the owner wallet’s transaction history since deployment to see whether it has been dormant or quietly active.
