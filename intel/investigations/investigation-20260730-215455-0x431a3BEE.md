## Expert Assessment
- Agrees with the verdict above:

The evidence paints AAPLB as a freshly launched, upgradeable proxy token on BSC that is explicitly branded around the AAPL/Apple ticker and trading at a price level that mirrors real-world equity. The combination of BeaconProxy verification, zero taxes, no honeypot flags, and 1656 holders initially suggests a functional DEX listing rather than an outright scam contract. However, that surface coherence collapses when the same deployer’s prior QQQB contract (also rejected) is factored in: the pattern points to a serial campaign of equity-mimicking tokens rather than an isolated project. The 0.7-day-old pair plus 93 % supply held by the top 10 non-LP wallets further indicates the liquidity and holder count are likely manufactured for optics, not organic distribution; the custom source code does not offset the structural ability of an upgradeable proxy to alter behavior post-launch.

The rule-based score correctly weights the proxy and serial-deployer risks but underweights the holder-concentration signal. A 93 % top-10 figure on a token that already shows 1656 addresses is not merely “concentrated”—it is incompatible with any credible claim of broad distribution and directly enables single-wallet price control regardless of the 1656-wallet headline number.

Next cycle should pull the implementation address behind the BeaconProxy and inspect its actual functions (especially any mint/upgrade or ownership-transfer logic) rather than stopping at the proxy metadata.
