- Agrees with the verdict above:

The evidence paints a picture of an old, low-activity BSC token (FatToken) that has sat on PancakeSwap for roughly 2.5 years with minimal trading interest. Renounced ownership and the absence of minting or honeypot flags create a surface-level impression of a “set and forget” contract that can no longer be altered by its deployer. However, the same data set shows zero holders, only 49 % of liquidity locked, and an active pause capability still coded into the contract. These points do not cohere into a healthy, distributed token; instead they suggest either a completely abandoned project whose remaining liquidity could still be removed or a shell whose on-paper renouncement has not eliminated every privileged function.

The rule-based score correctly flags the pause risk and the thin distribution, but it under-weights the tension between “ownership renounced” and “pause still possible.” Because the owner address is the zero address, the pause function is effectively dead today, yet the contract still exposes it; any future upgrade path or proxy pattern (even if currently absent) could re-introduce control. The 49 % liquidity lock is also more concerning than the numeric penalty implies: with only $94 k total liquidity and $240 daily volume, the unlocked half represents a material rug vector that a single large holder could exploit without needing to touch ownership.

Next step: pull the verified source and search the pause-related functions plus any liquidity-removal or fee-recipient setters to confirm whether renouncement truly neutralizes them; simultaneously verify the exact lock contract and unlock schedule for the remaining 51 % of LP tokens.
