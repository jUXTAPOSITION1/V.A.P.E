## Expert Assessment
- Agrees with the verdict above:

The evidence paints QToken as a minimal, anonymous BSC token with a renounced (or never-set) owner, zero mint capability, and zero taxes—yet deployed with a hand-written contract rather than a cloned template. The 24-holder distribution and 81% top-10 concentration sit in direct tension with the unlocked liquidity: any single large holder can sell into thin pools without friction, and the absence of a pair-creation timestamp leaves open the possibility this is either brand-new or an older contract whose early activity was never recorded on the indexers. The custom source code is the only counter-signal, implying at least one developer bothered to write and verify something bespoke, but without any on-chain comments, events, or linked documentation it does not establish a product, team, or use case. Taken together, the picture is internally consistent with a low-effort anonymous launch that deliberately avoided the most obvious rug vectors (mint, proxy, honeypot) while leaving the classic manipulation vectors (concentration + unlocked LP) intact.

The rule-based score correctly weights the holder concentration and unlocked liquidity as dominant risks; those two factors alone explain the bulk of the 32/100. It may slightly overweight the lack of a pair timestamp, since the verified custom source and owner=None already constrain certain attack surfaces regardless of exact age. Conversely, it underweights the fact that the same custom source could contain hidden transfer restrictions or backdoors that token-safety data alone would not surface.

Next step: pull the verified source and scan for any non-standard transfer, fee, or access-control logic, then cross-check the top-10 holder addresses against recent on-chain activity to see whether they are exchange wallets, known bots, or single-entity clusters.
