## Expert Assessment
- Agrees with the verdict above:

The evidence paints AEON as a brand-new, anonymous BSC token with no disclosed utility, team, or roadmap. Its contract is custom and verified rather than a clone, which rules out the most obvious low-effort rugs, yet the combination of mintable supply, pausable transfers, and an apparently renounced-but-still-powerful owner creates a direct path for the deployer (or whoever retains those privileges) to expand supply or freeze movement after liquidity is added. The 89 % top-10 concentration sits in tension with the 10 513-holder count: the latter looks reassuring until one notes that the same wallets could have been seeded via airdrop or bot activity to manufacture distribution optics while the actual float remains tiny. The 24 h volume of $1.36 M against only $21.5 k liquidity is another internal contradiction—such turnover on sub-$25 k depth is statistically improbable without coordinated trading or wash activity, especially on a pair that is less than half a day old.

The rule-based score correctly flags the structural risks but appears to overweight the raw holder count as a positive while under-weighting how quickly that count could have been gamed on a 0.4-day-old launch; conversely, it may under-weight the verified custom source code, which at least raises the bar for a simple exit-scam template.

Next step: pull the top-20 holder addresses and their transaction histories to test whether the distribution is organic or the result of a small number of wallets receiving and immediately redistributing large allocations.
