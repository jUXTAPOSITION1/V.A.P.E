The combination of 29k+ holders, zero buy/sell taxes, non-honeypot status, and a custom (non-factory) verified contract of 7.6 kB size is consistent with an established token rather than a fresh deployment; the 70 % 24 h price swing and $570 k volume on modest $144 k liquidity further align with an older asset that can still see retail-driven volatility without having been pulled.  

The three flagged risks do not contradict that history but do sit in tension with it: mintability remains enabled, the same owner address that deployed the contract still holds control, and no liquidity is reported as locked. These are not artifacts of a new rug; they are structural features that have persisted across 2,600+ days of trading, which either indicates the team never fully relinquished upgrade or issuance rights or that the project has simply never needed to renounce them. The data therefore coheres into a legitimate but not fully decentralized legacy token rather than a scam, with the longevity metrics outweighing the static risk flags.

The rule-based score overweighted the liquidity-lock and owner-renounce items relative to the actual age and holder distribution; for a contract this old, those two items are more diagnostic of governance posture than of imminent exit risk, yet the scoring treats them identically to the same flags on a one-week-old token. It underweighted the independent corroboration from DefiLlama’s multi-year pricing feed, which is harder to fake than any single on-chain metric.

Next step: pull the mint function’s access-control history (or any recent mint events) on the verified contract to see whether the owner address has actually exercised mint rights in the last 12–24 months and whether any timelock or multisig now gates it.
