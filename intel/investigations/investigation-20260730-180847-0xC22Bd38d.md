The evidence describes a Polygon-based ERC20 (named CES, implemented via a DropERC20 template) that has accumulated 8.6k holders and half a million in liquidity over roughly three months of trading, with zero taxes and no honeypot flags. Those surface metrics initially suggest a live, traded asset rather than a one-day mint-and-dump. However, the same data set shows the entire non-LP supply sitting in the top ten wallets, zero liquidity locked, and an explicit link to a prior CES deployment by the identical creator that already carried a REJECT label. The rule-based score correctly flags the upgradeability and concentration risks but appears to overweight the “98+ days without incident” signal while underweighting the direct deployer linkage. The prior CES at 0x3f234f3Ab2B79dcF243Ab387939d2F7e2530b676 was rejected on essentially identical grounds; treating the current token as an isolated case therefore misses the campaign-level pattern. In addition, the evidence contains an internal contradiction—token-safety data marks the contract as a proxy while the verified source record states proxy=False—which is not resolved by any on-chain code-size or implementation details provided here.
