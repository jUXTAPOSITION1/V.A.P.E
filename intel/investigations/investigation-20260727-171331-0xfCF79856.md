The evidence paints RWA as a long-dormant, low-activity token that has survived on-chain for roughly 2.2 years with the classic post-2022 “renounce-and-lock” safety pattern. Ownership at the zero address, 100 % locked liquidity, verified non-proxy source, and independent pricing history on DefiLlama all line up to indicate the deployer walked away after launch rather than retaining mint or upgrade keys. The 722-holder base and modest 0.05 % taxes are consistent with that picture. However, the same data set shows extreme concentration (top-10 wallets holding 84 % outside LP/burn) paired with essentially dead trading ($1 daily volume, $18 k liquidity). This combination suggests either a handful of early insiders still control the float or the token simply became illiquid and forgotten; either way, price discovery is absent and any large holder can still move the market.

The rule-based score overweighted the “pausable by owner” flag. Because the owner address is explicitly zero, that control path is closed; the flag therefore double-counts a risk that the renouncement evidence already neutralizes. Conversely, the score under-weighted the liquidity-and-volume collapse: 837 days of trading history is only meaningful if volume ever existed, yet current on-chain activity is near zero, which amplifies the manipulation risk already flagged by holder concentration.

Next step: pull the actual holder list (top 20) and cross-reference their first-acquisition timestamps against the contract’s deployment block to determine whether the concentrated wallets are original team/insider addresses or later accumulators.
