### LG Electronics USA Cracks Down on Smart TV Apps Turning Devices into Residential Proxies

LG Electronics USA has announced plans to suspend smart TV apps that turn users' televisions into always-on residential proxy nodes. This move comes less than a month after research by the security firm [Spur](https://spur.us/blog/smart-tv-apps-residential-proxy-sdks) found that more than 42 percent of games and other apps available for download on LG's webOS store include residential proxy software development kits (SDKs).

## A Significant Issue in Smart TV Ecosystem

The research, published on July 2, 2026, exposed the deep penetration of proxy monetization software in the smart TV ecosystem. According to the findings, these SDKs turn the television's internet connection into an active relay node for external, unknown traffic. Samsung's Tizen operating system also faces a similar issue, with more than a quarter of its available smart TV apps containing proxy SDKs.

## LG's Response to the Findings

In response to the research, LG Senior Vice President John Taylor confirmed to [Krebs on Security](https://krebsonsecurity.com/2026/07/lg-to-ban-residential-proxies-from-smart-tv-apps/) that the company is working with app developers to remove the residential proxy option from their apps on the webOS platform. Taylor stated, "A residential proxy network is not an intended use for LG smart TVs, and LG Electronics is working with developers to remove the residential proxy option from their apps on the webOS platform. If this option is not removed, these apps will be suspended." He also mentioned that a review of the webOS app catalog is "well underway now" and that LG will continue to strengthen its evaluation process for developer-submitted apps.

## The Economics Behind Residential Proxies

The proliferation of proxy SDKs is driven by a monetization model where app developers are paid by proxy providers to bundle these SDKs into their code. The proxy providers then aggregate these residential IP addresses and rent them to clients. While some buyers use residential proxies for legitimate purposes, they are also used by cybercriminals to mask malicious activities, such as launching credential-stuffing attacks or coordinating botnet campaigns.

## Potential Consequences for Consumers

For consumers, having a television converted into a proxy node can lead to degraded network performance, increased bandwidth consumption, and the risk of having their residential IP addresses blacklisted. In severe cases, a hijacked TV could route illegal traffic through a user's home router, potentially drawing law enforcement scrutiny.

## Samsung's Position

Unlike LG, Samsung has not announced a similar policy for its Tizen OS ecosystem, despite over 25 percent of its TV apps being found to contain the same proxy SDKs. The lack of unified industry standards for smart TV app stores leaves millions of household devices vulnerable to exploitation.

## Protecting Your Home Network

To secure your home network, consider the following steps:
* Review and uninstall unnecessary apps on your smart TV.
* Monitor network traffic for unusual activity.
* Perform a factory reset if you suspect your TV has been compromised.
* Set up a guest Wi-Fi network for smart TVs and other IoT devices to prevent potential attackers from accessing your primary network.
