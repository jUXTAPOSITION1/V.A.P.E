**WASHINGTON, D.C.** — The Securities and Exchange Commission has proposed Regulation E-Delivery, a new rule that would expand the ability of issuers, broker-dealers, investment advisers, and others to use electronic delivery to satisfy information delivery requirements under the federal securities laws.

Announced on July 16, 2026, the proposed [Regulation E-Delivery](https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2026-67-sec-proposes-new-e-delivery-approach-make-information-more-readily-accessible-useful-investors) would allow required information to be delivered electronically without first obtaining affirmative consent. The proposal preserves the ability to receive delivery in paper format on request.

## Current Framework and Proposed Change

Under the current approach, required regulatory information is typically delivered in paper format unless the recipient affirmatively elects otherwise. Regulation E-Delivery would generally supersede the Commission’s decades-old, guidance-based e-delivery approach.

## Chairman’s Statement

In a [statement accompanying the proposal](http://www.sec.gov/newsroom/speeches-statements/atkins-statement-regulation-e-delivery-07-16-26), SEC Chairman Paul S. Atkins said: “Today, the Commission took an important step toward allowing the financial services industry to harness technology for the benefit of everyday American investors. By proposing to permit electronic delivery to become the default method for issuers, market intermediaries, and others to communicate with investors, we are taking another stride toward a regulatory framework suitable for the modern era, a key pillar of my agenda.”

Atkins added: “In an age of artificial intelligence and blockchain technology, a default to paper delivery should be a relic, not a standard.”

The SEC stated that the proposal reflects how today’s issuers, market intermediaries, investors, and others use electronic media and would provide savings to issuers, market intermediaries, and, ultimately, investors, in paper, printing, and postage costs.
