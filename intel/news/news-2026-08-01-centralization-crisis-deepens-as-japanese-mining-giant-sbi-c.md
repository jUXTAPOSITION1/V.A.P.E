The consolidation of Bitcoin's global hashrate has reached a critical milestone following the quiet exit of one of the network's most prominent institutional operators. SBI Crypto, the mining subsidiary of Japanese financial conglomerate SBI Group, has officially terminated its Bitcoin mining pool service after a month-long wind-down. 

This exit comes at a precarious moment for the network's decentralization. Even before SBI Crypto completely pulled the plug, the top three Bitcoin mining pools had already consolidated more than 60% of the network's total computing power.

## What Happened

According to on-chain mining data tracked throughout July 2026, SBI Crypto underwent a rapid and drastic reduction in its mining footprint. The pool's seven-day average hashrate fell from 16.222 exahashes per second (EH/s) on June 30 to just 5.817 EH/s by July 30. 

By August 1, 2026, the service was officially discontinued. The sudden drop-off and ultimate closure of the pool have left a noticeable void in the network's distribution of computing power. 

Aggregate pool records do not show where SBI Crypto’s lost hashrate moved.

## Key Statements

The shifting dynamics of the network are laid bare in the weekly hashrate distribution metrics. According to data published by [CryptoSlate](https://cryptoslate.com/a-major-japanese-bitcoin-mining-pool-just-pulled-the-plug-on-its-bitcoin-service-just-as-3-mega-miners-claimed-60-of-the-network/), "the top three pools already held 64.8% in the July 20 weekly bucket, before SBI’s cutoff." 

This means that even before SBI Crypto's 5.8 EH/s was removed from the active pool registry, a mere trio of mining entities controlled nearly two-thirds of the entire Bitcoin network's security.
