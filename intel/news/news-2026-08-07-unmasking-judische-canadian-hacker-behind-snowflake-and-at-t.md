Connor Riley Moucka, a 26-year-old software engineer from Kitchener, Ontario, has pleaded guilty to computer fraud and conspiracy to hack and extort more than 165 organizations that used the Snowflake cloud data platform. Operating under the aliases “Judische” and “Waifu,” Moucka also admitted to stealing call and text history records of more than 100 million AT&T customers.

The plea brings a formal end to the run of a threat actor once described by investigators as one of the most consequential cybercriminals of 2024. According to the U.S. Department of Justice, Moucka and his co-conspirators operated between February and October 2024, extracting ransom payments from major corporations by threatening to dump stolen customer data online.

## A Campaign of Corporate Chaos

The conspiracy targeted Snowflake customer accounts that did not enforce multi-factor authentication. Moucka and his associates used stolen login credentials to access corporate Snowflake databases.

Once inside, the hackers exfiltrated large volumes of data. The campaign affected companies including Ticketmaster, LendingTree, Advance Auto Parts, and Neiman Marcus. In response, Snowflake increased password complexity requirements and enforced multi-factor authentication for its customers.

## The AT&T Loot and Extortion Machine

Beyond the Snowflake breaches, Moucka admitted to the theft of call and text history records belonging to more than 100 million AT&T customers. Federal prosecutors stated that the conspirators downloaded terabytes of information, including non-content call and text history records, banking and other financial information, payroll records, Drug Enforcement Administration (DEA) registration numbers, driver’s license numbers, passport numbers, social security numbers and other personally identifiable information.

The Justice Department said the conspirators made over $2.5 million in ransom payments. Moucka also threatened and harassed government officials and security researchers who were helping to track him down.

## The Extremist Cyber-Underworld

Moucka’s online identity as “Judische” was first documented in a September 2024 KrebsOnSecurity story that examined the overlap between Western, English-speaking cybercriminals and extremist groups that harass and extort minors into harming themselves or others. That story identified Judische as a software engineer from Ontario who had been involved in numerous data breaches and voice phishing attacks against U.S. companies since at least 2020.

## Surveillance, Arrest, and Justice

Canadian authorities arrested Moucka on a provisional warrant from the United States a little more than a month after the September 2024 story. An RCMP affidavit included a surveillance photo of Moucka dated October 21, 2024, nine days before his arrest.

With his guilty plea, Moucka now faces significant federal prison time in the United States.
