## What Happened

Russia has added Moscow, the Moscow Region and parts of the Kursk Region to its cryptocurrency mining ban list. Resolution No. 936, signed by Prime Minister Mikhail Mishustin on July 25, 2026, amends an earlier December 2024 mining restriction order. The updated rules add these areas to the list of restricted zones, with the ban set to take effect on Aug. 15, 2026, and remain in place through Dec. 31, 2032, according to records published on [Pravo.ru](http://publication.pravo.gov.ru/document/0001202607310027). The measure covers eight municipal districts and the city of Lgov in the Kursk Region.

## Key Statements

The Moscow Region’s Energy Ministry previously said a mining ban was needed because of growing electricity demand, according to a report by [TASS](https://tass.ru/ekonomika/27972867). The ministry estimated that Moscow and the Moscow Region have 65 data centers connected to the power grid with a combined capacity of 734 megawatts (MW), including 19 data centers in the Moscow Region with 233 MW of capacity.

Similar restrictions were [introduced](http://publication.pravo.gov.ru/document/0001202603200051) in parts of Buryatia and the Zabaykalsky Krai, where a mining ban is set to run from April 1, 2026, through March 15, 2031.

## Context & Impact

The expansion applies existing power-supply restrictions to additional territories. No further details on enforcement, relocation effects, or secondary market activity are provided in the source material.
