TeraWulf reported second-quarter digital-asset revenue of just $12.8 million, a 73 percent decline from $47.6 million a year earlier, while high-performance computing leases generated $31.9 million and accounted for 71 percent of total sales.

The company’s overall revenue reached $44.8 million, still roughly 6 percent below the prior-year level because the new AI-related contracts did not fully replace the lost mining income. A $940.8 million net loss accompanied the shift, reflecting heavy spending on data-center expansion that remains incomplete.

## Mining Revenue Collapse and HPC Surge

The Lake Mariner campus in New York continues to host Bitcoin-mining hardware, but portions are being converted for contracted HPC workloads. This reallocation has already reduced mining to a secondary activity.

HPC leasing revenue of $31.9 million arrived in the same quarter that digital-asset income fell to $12.8 million, according to the firm’s earnings release covered by CryptoSlate. The 71 percent contribution from AI-related leases marks the clearest evidence yet that TeraWulf’s power capacity is being redirected away from proof-of-work operations.

## Capital Demands and Construction Pipeline

Building the infrastructure required for the pivot produced the $940.8 million quarterly loss. The company still has 336 MW of capacity under construction, with meaningful revenue from its announced Anthropic contract not expected until 2027.

Chief Financial Officer Patrick Fleury characterized the period as “another step in the transformation of our financial profile,” citing stronger credit support behind the new leases. The statement appears in the same CryptoSlate report that detailed the revenue split.
