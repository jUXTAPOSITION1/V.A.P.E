Russian retailers recorded strong growth in hardware wallet demand during the first half of 2026, with M.Video reporting unit sales more than doubling from the prior quarter and Wildberries posting an 84% year-over-year increase, data that coincides with the countdown to Russia's new crypto framework taking effect September 1.

M.Video said its hardware-wallet units rose 107% in the second quarter compared with the first, while sales value climbed 92%. Wildberries reported an 84% increase in units sold during the first half versus the same period in 2025, alongside a 60% rise in value, according to figures cited by RIA Novosti from parent company RWB. Neither retailer disclosed absolute unit totals or named specific models driving the gains.

## Retailer-Level Trends
The two marketplaces operate on different reporting cycles, which complicates direct comparison. M.Video tracked sequential growth from Q1 to Q2 2026, while Wildberries measured first-half results against the prior year. Both point to acceleration in physical-device purchases.

Wildberries also cut its average selling price by 13% to 7,900 rubles during the period, and M.Video expanded the number of wallet models it carries. Neither company identified the precise catalyst, though the timing aligns with the final months before the September 1 regime change.

## Regulatory Timeline and Mechanics
Russia's incoming rules bar withdrawals from licensed digital depositories to personal wallets after a transition period ending July 1, 2027. From September 1, 2026 onward, crypto transactions must route through regulated exchanges and depositories, with retail investors limited to a 300,000-ruble annual purchase cap per intermediary after passing a knowledge test. Domestic crypto payments remain prohibited.

Lawyers quoted by RBC confirmed that non-custodial hardware wallets themselves stay legal; the restrictions target flows between regulated platforms and private devices. Hardware wallets store private keys offline, reducing online-service exposure, yet they introduce their own seed-phrase and firmware risks.

## Lingering Technical Vulnerabilities
Even as demand rises, device-level security issues persist. On July 30, Coinkite disclosed a Coldcard firmware flaw that compromised seed generation, an incident tied to estimated losses exceeding $116 million across affected users. The episode underscores that hardware solutions do not eliminate all custody hazards, particularly during the setup phase.
