HEADLINE: AI Firms' Book-Shredding Spree Raises Alarms Over Copyright Law and Preservation Incentives
DEK: The quiet destruction of physical books by AI firms, fueled by a 2025 fair use ruling, threatens the preservation of rare titles and highlights the need for revised copyright laws.

## Introduction to the Crisis
The recent [report by CryptoSlate](https://cryptoslate.com/why-copyright-law-is-quietly-rewarding-ai-firms-for-shredding-physical-books/) has shed light on a disturbing trend: AI firms are shredding physical books to feed their machine learning models, taking advantage of a loophole in copyright law. This practice, while not entirely new, has gained momentum following a 2025 ruling that treated [Anthropic's destructive scanning of purchased books as fair use](https://cryptoslate.com/why-copyright-law-is-quietly-rewarding-ai-firms-for-shredding-physical-books/).

## The Role of ISBNdb and AI Developers
[ISBNdb](https://cryptoslate.com/why-copyright-law-is-quietly-rewarding-ai-firms-for-shredding-physical-books/), a platform that markets physical-book orders of up to one million titles to AI developers, plays a significant role in this crisis. While there is no title-level evidence to suggest that a rare book was destroyed, the lack of preservation incentives is a pressing concern.

## The Fair Use Ruling and Its Consequences
The 2025 fair use ruling, which allowed Anthropic to scan and destroy purchased books without repercussions, has set a troubling precedent. As [CryptoSlate](https://cryptoslate.com/why-copyright-law-is-quietly-rewarding-ai-firms-for-shredding-physical-books/) notes, the lack of title-level evidence does not diminish the significance of this issue.
