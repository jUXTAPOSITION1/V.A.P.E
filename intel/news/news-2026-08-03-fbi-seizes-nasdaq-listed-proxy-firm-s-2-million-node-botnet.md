### The Federal Bureau of Investigation (FBI) Seizes NetNut Domains

The Federal Bureau of Investigation (FBI) has seized hundreds of domains associated with NetNut, a residential proxy service operated by Alarum Technologies [NASDAQ: ALAR], a publicly traded Israeli company. This action is linked to the dismantling of the Popa botnet, a network of compromised devices. 

The seizure occurred roughly two weeks after cybersecurity researchers publicly linked NetNut’s infrastructure to illicit botnet operations. Visitors to NetNut’s homepage are now greeted by a joint seizure notice from the FBI and the Internal Revenue Service Criminal Investigation (IRS-CI) division.

## The Corporate Botnet Connection

NetNut was found to populate its proxy pool with devices compromised by the Popa botnet. This botnet silently infects consumer electronics, such as smart TVs and streaming boxes, with malicious software, turning them into always-on proxy nodes without the owners' knowledge or consent. NetNut then rented access to this network to third parties, who used the residential IPs for abusive and intrusive internet activities.

## Fueling Global Cybercrime

According to the [Google Threat Intelligence Group (GTIG)](https://cloud.google.com/blog/topics/threat-intelligence/google-continued-disruption-residential-proxy-networks), NetNut’s proxy network was widely resold and white-labeled by third-party proxy providers. This made NetNut's services attractive to cybercriminals and espionage groups seeking to obfuscate their attacks. 

In a single week during June 2026, Google's threat researchers observed 316 distinct threat actor clusters using suspected NetNut exit nodes. 

"These bad actors can use NetNut to mask their origin IP address when accessing victim environments, accessing their own infrastructure, and conducting password spray attacks," Google's GTIG wrote. When a consumer device becomes an exit node, it exposes the victim's home network to unauthorized traffic.

## A Broad Coalition of Industry Partners

The FBI's seizure notice credited Google, Lumen, and Shadowserver for their assistance in dismantling the Popa botnet's infrastructure, highlighting the importance of public-private partnerships in combatting cyber-mercenary networks.

## What to Watch Next

The downfall of NetNut mirrors previous law enforcement actions against residential proxy networks that operated as fronts for malware-fueled botnets. Alarum Technologies faces regulatory, legal, and financial jeopardy following the seizure of its primary revenue-generating infrastructure.

For consumers, the NetNut takedown is a reminder of smart home device security vulnerabilities. Security experts recommend auditing and securing home networks to ensure devices have not been conscripted into similar botnet operations.

* **Audit Connected Devices:** Check your router's admin console to view all connected devices. Look for unrecognized devices drawing unusual amounts of upload bandwidth.
* **Update Firmware:** Ensure all smart home devices, routers, and streaming boxes are running the latest security patches.
* **Segment Home Networks:** Utilize your router's "guest network" feature to isolate IoT and smart home devices from primary computers and phones.
