A wallet holding $36 million in stolen bitcoin has become an unlikely public message board, as hack victims and opportunists pay to leave permanent notes for the thief.

The CoinDesk report details how the address, linked to the Coldcard hack, now hosts a growing collection of on-chain inscriptions ranging from direct appeals like "You stole, please return some" to more elaborate hustles from third parties offering recovery services or demanding cuts.

## The Mechanics of the Message Board

Bitcoin's ability to attach small data payloads via inscriptions or OP_RETURN outputs has allowed anyone to write to the thief's address by sending tiny amounts and embedding text. Each message costs the sender a transaction fee, creating a low-friction but permanent record that cannot be edited or removed.

Early reports from the CoinDesk coverage indicate the wallet still holds the full stolen amount, suggesting the thief has not moved funds despite the visible public pressure. No official statement from the hacker or any law-enforcement recovery effort appears in the available reporting.

## Who Is Writing and Why

The messages come from two distinct groups. Direct victims of the Coldcard exploit post short, often emotional requests for partial restitution. A second cohort of opportunists uses the address to advertise "recovery" schemes, demand finder’s fees, or attempt social-engineering plays.

Because every inscription is timestamped and tied to a specific transaction, the record provides an immutable log of who tried to contact the thief and when. CoinDesk notes that this has turned the address into a de-facto public forum rather than a private negotiation channel.

## What to Watch Next
