Bipartisan negotiators have sent newly drafted ethics language for the long-awaited crypto Clarity Act to the White House for review, but a looming five-week congressional recess has thrown the bill's immediate legislative future into uncertainty.

The compromise text was drafted and sent to the White House last week. However, procedural roadblocks and a rapidly closing legislative window mean the bill is running out of time to reach the Senate floor before the break.

## The Race Against the Recess Clock

The Senate is scheduled to begin its five-week break on Thursday or Friday. This deadline has forced negotiators into a race against time.

The odds of a pre-recess vote fell on Wednesday afternoon, August 5, when Senate Majority Leader John Thune declined to file cloture on the crypto market structure bill.

Thune's decision leaves negotiators with a narrow window to reach consensus. While Thune had told reporters earlier on Wednesday that he still hoped to see a vote before the recess, Senate leadership ultimately chose to prioritize other legislative items.

## Bipartisan Ethics Negotiations Behind Closed Doors

Despite the procedural setbacks, bipartisan negotiations have intensified. Senator Thom Tillis, who has been working with Democrats on ethics language for the bill, confirmed that the White House is actively engaged in reviewing the latest compromise language.

"We’ve got people working with the White House right now…they’re going through some of the lines right now," Tillis said, in remarks first reported by *Punchbowl News* Senior Reporter Brendan Pedersen and covered by [*Bitcoin Magazine*](https://bitcoinmagazine.com/news/white-house-reviewing-crypto-clarity-act).

The sudden flurry of executive-level review follows weeks of lobbying from major industry players. Just last week, top cryptocurrency firms, including Coinbase, alongside several major traditional financial institutions, expressed public optimism that a bipartisan breakthrough on market structure was within reach.

## Partisan Rifts and Shifting Priorities

While Tillis and his Democratic counterparts have focused on polishing the ethics compromise, partisan tensions on the Senate floor remain high. Some Republican lawmakers have accused Democrats of intentionally stalling the legislation.

According to Capitol Hill reports, Republican and Democratic staff held a series of meetings over the prior 24 hours in a bid to resolve remaining differences. However, those efforts have clashed with the Senate's broader legislative agenda.

Tillis acknowledged that the viability of a vote now depends on whether the Senate stays in session past its scheduled Thursday departure.

"If we were getting out on schedule, which would be tomorrow afternoon, I think it’d be a moonshot at this point," Tillis said on Wednesday.
